Type

Tracking is in ‰: thousandths of an em, as in Adobe apps. Match size scales each font so its x-height or cap height meets the base style's; it exports as per-letter size. Letter align is measured by each glyph's outline: top, middle and bottom line up the actual ink of each letter within its line. They export as baseline shift, so the text stays editable.

Jitter

Size range, base jitter, track jitter and rotation jitter add a random amount to each letter, picked from the seed; a letter you rotated by hand keeps its own angle. Baseline jitter only moves letters set on the baseline.

Look

One click sets colors and decoration: palette, boxes and bands, pattern and marks. Your type stays as it is: text, fonts, mix, size, jitter, outline and letter edits. Change anything afterwards; ⌘Z goes back.

Color

A palette sets the text and background colors, and the box and band fill when it has a block color. Letters you colored by hand keep their color. The background fills the text block plus a margin, the same area the PNG export crops to. Palettes apply to the whole design: text, background, boxes, bands, and the swatch row under every color control. Click the active palette again for a new color match; fold the list to just its chips.

Paragraph band

Pick a palette with block colors first.

One band under each line of text, as tall as its tallest letter or stepping with each letter. Per line from palette gives each line the next of the palette's block and extra colors; Single uses the Fill under Box style.

Random letter boxes

Pattern

The pattern sits behind boxes and text and fills the text block plus a margin, the area the PNG crops to. It exports to the .jsx as editable vectors on its own layer; a pattern too fine (more than 4000 pieces) is left out of the .jsx.

Registration marks

Crosshairs at the four corners of the text block plus a margin, Inset in from each corner, so they are always inside the PNG. They export to the .jsx as editable vectors on their own layer.

Random outline

Solid ↔ outline. Random outline picks a share of the letters to set as outline only: no fill, a stroke in the text color. It exports as live text with a stroke. Select letters to set them by hand.

    Photoshop

    File › Scripts › Browse…. A new 72 ppi document opens with one editable type layer you can drag into your comp.

    Base style

    The base style is the one letters keep at 0% mix. Pick it from the Base menu, or right-click any style in the pool: Set as base style, or Add to mix + set as base for a style that is off. It must be switched on in the pool: styles that are off are greyed out in the list, and switching off the base moves it to the nearest weight that is on.

    Look

    The Look group, at the top of the left panel's Style tab, sets a whole look in one click, from settings that already exist: the palette, the paragraph band, random letter boxes, the pattern and registration marks. Riso Block, Stacked Bands, Tone on Tone and Dark Glow. A look only sets colors and decoration: your text, seed, fonts, mix, type settings, jitter, outline and letter edits stay as they are; random letter boxes from before are replaced, boxes you added or colored by hand stay. It is a starting point: change anything afterwards, and ⌘Z goes back.

    Specimen

    Specimen in the top bar keeps a whole look as settings: the text, every setting, your letter edits, the pool and base style, and the names of the fonts (family, style and PostScript name), not the fonts themselves. Copy specimen puts it on the clipboard; Download specimen saves a .json; give it a title and author if you like. To load one, paste it and press Load pasted, or Choose file. Loading is one step, so ⌘Z goes back. With the same fonts it gives exactly the same result. If some fonts aren't loaded, Mixweight lists them, lets the base style or your other fonts stand in, and the layout will differ because their widths differ. Everything works offline; nothing is uploaded.

    Seed

    Every random choice comes from the seed, so the same seed and settings always give the same result. Click Seed in the toolbar to type one, Enter to apply, Esc to cancel. Note a seed to get a layout back later.

    Fonts

    Fonts are matched by family and style name, then by PostScript name. Variable fonts use their named instances (Light, Bold…), so the app shows what the preview shows. Fonts the app can't find are listed and swapped for its default; the text stays editable.

    Family share

    Share of mix is each family's part of the letters that leave the base style. At first it follows the number of styles switched on. Drag one family and the others make room, keeping their proportions, so the total stays 100%. Styles keep their place in the light → heavy range.

    Distribution

    Ramp sets light → heavy from the start of the text to the end; Ramp per line restarts on every line. Alternate switches between the lighter and heavier half of the pool. Wave has a Period. Weight mix still decides how many letters leave the base style.

    Even gaps

    Where neighbouring letters come from different fonts, their sidebearings differ and the gaps look uneven. Even gaps adjusts the left letter's tracking so the gap moves halfway between the spacing of the two fonts it joins, instead of jumping from one to the other. It exports as tracking.

    Match size

    Different families at the same point size look different sizes. x-height or Cap height scales each letter's font so its lowercase x or capital H is as tall as in the base style (limited to 60–160%). It is written as the letter's point size, so it stays editable; leading follows the nominal size.

    Letter align

    Top, middle and bottom alignment are written as baseline shift values. If you change a letter or its font afterwards, export again to re-align.

    Group titles

    Click a group title in the left panel to fold the group to its title row (▸) or open it again (▾); each group remembers its own state. Folding Font pool hides every family; a family's name folds that family to one row, which still shows its share of mix. The left panel has two tabs: Type (font pool, text, mix, type with letter align, jitter) and Style (look, color, paragraph band, random letter boxes, pattern, registration marks, random outline). On a first visit Jitter and the palette list start folded; once you fold or open them yourself, your choice stays. Paragraph band, Pattern and Registration marks show only Off / On until you switch them on. The right side of each title row sums up what the group is set to, and follows every change; hover it for the full text if it is cut short. Groups with a short note have a ? next to their title: click it to show the note, again to hide it. Clear, next to the tabs, sets everything in the left panel (both tabs) back to its default and removes the boxes Random letter boxes placed; your text, fonts, seed, guides and right-panel edits stay.

    Right panel

    Selecting letters opens it with their font, size, shift and tracking at the top, then weight, align, rotation, fill and boxes. With nothing selected it folds to a narrow Selection strip on the right edge, and the canvas takes the room; deselecting folds it again. Close › folds it while letters stay selected, until you select other letters; the strip's ‹ opens it. Settings for the whole text are in the left panel's Style tab: Random outline (with Stroke), the Paragraph band and Random letter boxes. Clear edits removes what you set on selected letters, and bands; the left panel's Clear resets the left panel's settings and removes the boxes Random letter boxes placed. A drag that starts on empty canvas selects the letters it sweeps over. The panel keeps its place: selecting other letters, or deselecting and reselecting, brings back the group you were using. Right-click a letter (Ctrl+click on a Mac) for a small panel beside the selection with the controls used most: weight, angle, fill, letter color (outline letters stroke in it) with Use text color, letter boxes, box fill, band, Reroll, Reset and Deselect. They are the right panel's own controls, so a change in one shows in the other. Esc or a click elsewhere closes it; right-click on empty canvas keeps the browser's menu. Hover info in the bar above the canvas sets the hover tag: Off, Basic (family, style, size, shift and tracking) or Full, which adds a line for each thing that applies to the letter: its box fit and fill, bands, blend mode, rotation and fill (solid or outline). Canvas only, never exported.

    Boxes

    Letter boxes follow each letter's ink, size and shift, and turn with it. A band runs under the selection, as tall as its tallest letter or stepping with each one; the paragraph band does the same for every line. There are two Box style editors. The one in the left panel, folded under Random letter boxes, styles the whole text: the paragraph band, or every letter box (Paragraph band / Letter boxes). The one in the right panel styles the boxes and bands on the selected letters. A letter box you styled in the right panel, or whose fill you set by hand, keeps its style: the left editor and Reroll boxes leave it alone; Clear edits removes it. In Box style, Expand X and Y make a box wider or taller than the ink (negative values make it smaller), and Move X and Y shift it right and up (all four snap to 0 when you drag close to the tick at 0); a rotated letter's box moves along the letter's own axes. Letter boxes also have Height (Glyph: the letter's own ink; Line: as tall as the line's tallest letter) and Width (Glyph: the letter as set, without tracking; Advance: up to the next letter, so neighbouring boxes touch). Line + Advance makes a grid of columns. A letter can have a letter box and a band at once: when the selection has both, Box style shows Letter boxes / Band to pick which one its controls change (Add letter boxes and Add band switch to what they just added). With Each corner, the four corners sit in a grid laid out like the box: type a radius, or drag the small corner icon sideways, and pick Round, Bevel or Inverse under it. They export under the text as vector shapes: InDesign rectangles keep live corner options, Illustrator and Photoshop get the exact path.

    Color

    Box fill, box stroke and the background also take No color: the crossed-out swatch after the hex field (or type none). A box with no fill shows only its stroke; a background with no color leaves the PNG transparent. In the .jsx export a box with no fill keeps just its stroke (no fill in Illustrator and InDesign, a shape layer with its fill switched off in Photoshop); a box with neither fill nor stroke is left out. The Color group in the left panel's Style tab sets the text and background colors for the whole design, as RGB hex: click the big swatch for the system picker, type a hex value and press Enter, or click a small swatch (the current color's swatch is outlined). The row under each color holds the last eight colors used; with a palette active, a row of the palette's colors sits above it, under every color control. A palette sets text and background in one click, and the box and band fill too when it has a block color. Click the active palette again to roll a new color match from its colors (text that stays readable on the background, and a block color that shows on it); ⌘Z goes back. The palette list starts as a row of chips; open it with the arrow next to Palettes to see the names, and Mixweight remembers your choice. Black on white goes back to the start. Selected letters get their own color under Fill in the right panel (Use text color removes it); outline letters stroke in their color. Box style has Fill and Stroke colors and a blend mode (Normal, Multiply, Screen, Overlay, Difference), which mixes a box with the background and the boxes under it, never with the text. The background fills the text block plus a margin, the same area the PNG crops to. The guidelines' color is under Guides in the bar above the canvas. Random letter boxes have a Box color: Single gives every box its own fill; From palette gives each randomly placed box a color from the active palette's block and extra colors, picked by the seed, so Reroll boxes changes placement and colors together. A box whose fill you set by hand keeps it, and Reroll leaves it in place. From palette needs a palette with a block color. Everything exports: text color per letter, RGB fills and strokes (neutral greys stay greys, a Black tint in InDesign), blend modes, and the guideline color. A background that isn't white becomes a rectangle at the very back, named Mixweight background, on its own layer (Illustrator, InDesign) or as the bottom shape layer (Photoshop). InDesign adds each RGB color as a swatch named Mixweight #RRGGBB.

    Outline and rotation

    Outline letters export as live text with no fill and a stroke in the letter's color; rotation as character rotation, about the centre of the em box. Photoshop can do neither per letter inside one type layer, so those letters become their own type layers (outline as a layer-style stroke) and the main layer keeps a space of the same width.

    Guidelines

    Baseline, x-height, cap height and ascender of the base style at the set size. Tick Include in export under Guides (the bar above the canvas) to export them as plain lines on their own layer or group, named Mixweight guidelines.

    Guides and Decoration

    Guides in the bar above the canvas opens the guidelines: baseline, x-height, cap height and ascender of the base style, each on its own switch, their color, and Include in export. It reads On while any guideline shows; click outside or press Esc to close it. The Pattern and Registration marks groups are in the left panel's Style tab; each shows just Off / On until you switch it on. The pattern (Grid, Dots, Crosses, Lines, Diagonal) is drawn behind the boxes and the text, in the text block plus a margin, the area the PNG crops to; cells start at that area's top-left corner. Cell size runs from 2 to 120 pt. Dots have a Dot size as a share of the cell (100% and neighbouring dots touch), so they stay visible at any cell size; the other types have a Line weight. Box blend modes mix with it. Registration marks are crosshairs at the four corners of that same area: Size, Line weight, Inset (how far in from each corner) and Color; on a small area they stop at the middle. Pattern and marks show on the canvas and in the PNG (always inside it). They export to the .jsx as editable vectors on their own layers (Mixweight pattern, Mixweight marks); a pattern with more than 4000 pieces is too fine and is left out of the .jsx (make the Cell size larger, or use PNG).

    Layers

    The .jsx puts each part on its own layer, bottom to top: Mixweight background, Mixweight pattern, Mixweight boxes, Mixweight marks, Mixweight text, Mixweight guidelines (Illustrator and InDesign layers; Photoshop shape layers, with the text layers in a Mixweight text group). Exporting again into the same document reuses those layers. Only the parts you use are made.

    PNG

    PNG in the top bar saves a picture of the text and its boxes exactly as on the canvas, cropped to the text block plus a margin, without selection marks, hover or zoom. Pick the scale (1× is one pixel per point), a transparent background, and whether the guidelines that are switched on come along (separate from Include in export). Download it, or copy it to the clipboard; if the browser won't copy, it downloads instead. It is a flat image, not editable text. Chrome or Edge.
